# Output Contract

The human doesn't read walls of text. Every substantial answer has two layers; the investigation doesn't shrink, it changes address.

## Layer 1 — verdict (mandatory)

At most 5 lines, in plain business language: what happened, why, who or how much it affects, what to do. No file paths, field names, jargon, or narration of the investigation. The test: could a non-engineer understand it? A term you can't avoid gets a definition of ≤12 words on first use.

## Layer 2 — evidence (mandatory, below, labeled)

Root cause with `file:line`, IDs, the queries and numbers from the verification, the options you discarded with a one-line reason each.

## Never compress

Literal error messages · security warnings · confirmation of a destructive action · a command or query ready to paste.

## Chat ergonomics

- At most **one question per turn**. Decisions with an obvious answer you take and declare.
- Direction decisions go as a **numbered list** in the chat — options plus your recommendation marked — not a form.
- A working session opens with the picture in ≤5 lines.
- A large deliverable goes to a file with a short summary in chat.
- Backlog items: title = verb + result; one truth per item (today's state; the history goes in the doc).

## State the deliverable first

The human asks for the _artifact_, not the process. At the start of a large request, write one line: `Deliverable: <artifact> in <format>`. A skill invoked means delivering its product (a prompt skill delivers a prompt, not the result of running it). Research is a means, never the end. Put the final artifact in the "done means" criteria, not just the intermediate files.
